⚠️ CRITICAL BIOS REQUIREMENTS

Before proceeding, you MUST configure your BIOS settings correctly. Failure to do so will result in boot failures or Blue Screens (BSOD). Read the steps below carefully.

1

Disable Secure Boot

Restart your PC and press Del or F2 to enter BIOS. Go to the Boot/Security tab and set Secure Boot to DISABLED.

Why? The spoofer patches the bootmgfw.efi file. If Secure Boot is enabled, the motherboard will reject the modified file and fail to boot.

2

Enable CPU Virtualization

While in BIOS, ensure hardware virtualization is turned ON. The spoofer requires this to load the hypervisor driver without crashing your system.

  • Intel CPUs: Enable Intel Virtualization Technology (VT-x)
  • AMD CPUs: Enable SVM Mode (AMD-V)
3

Run as Administrator

Once your PC has restarted, right-click the loader application and select "Run as Administrator". Wait for the "Preparing..." phase to finish. Accept the UAC (User Account Control) prompt when it appears.

4

Automatic System Preparation

The spoofer automatically applies critical fixes in the background to prevent BSODs:

  • Disables Windows Fast Startup & Hibernation.
  • Resolves Hyper-V / Core Isolation (HVCI) conflicts.
  • Freezes the Windows Time Service to lock the RTC clock.
5

Restart & Verify

When the spoofer finishes installing the EFI modules, restart your computer. Upon booting back up, your HWID, TPM, and Disk serial numbers will be permanently spoofed at the hypervisor level.

If something goes wrong (which is highly likely if BIOS settings are ignored), do not fully trust the UNK EFI Spoofer. DO NOT FORMAT YOUR PC. The solution is quite simple. Follow the steps below to fix your bootloader.

Step 1: Prepare a Windows installation USB drive and boot your PC from it.

Step 2: When the Windows Setup screen appears, press SHIFT + F10 to open the Command Prompt (CMD).

Step 3: Enter the following commands sequentially:

diskpart
list volume

:: Find the EFI partition (usually FAT32 format)

exit

:: Rebuild the UEFI boot files

IMPORTANT: Replace X with the number of your EFI partition. Also, ensure that C:\Windows is the correct drive letter where your Windows is actually installed. Once completed, restart your PC and Windows will boot normally.
